M02 · Chapter 2

Bond Features

Par Value - The par value of a bond (also referred to as face value) is the principal amount of the bond that the issuer promises to repay to the bondholder at a future date.

Coupon Rate - A bond's coupon rate is the annual interest rate a bond issuer pays to the bondholder for a particular maturity, usually expressed as a percentage of its face value.

Maturity - A bond's maturity is the date when the bond issuer pays back the bond's principal and interest to the bondholder, and the bond's debt obligation ends.

Yield to Maturity - Yield to maturity is the total rate of return a bondholder can expect to earn on a bond if they hold it to maturity. It is the bonds internal rate of return.

M02 · Chapter 2

Why an index divisor must be adjusted · 1/2

In a price-weighted index, such as the Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA), the divisor serves as the denominator in the mathematical calculation used to determine the index's value.

Calculating the Average: The divisor is the number by which the sum of the individual share prices of the component stocks is divided. This calculation converts the total price sum into a standardized index figure.

Weighting Mechanism: By functioning as the denominator in a price-weighted average, the divisor facilitates a calculation where the index value is derived directly from the stock prices.

This structure means that higher-priced stocks have a greater impact on the index's movement than lower-priced stocks.

M02 · Chapter 2

Why an index divisor must be adjusted · 2/2

It is modified when corporate actions occur—such as stock splits, spinoffs, or dividend payments—to ensure that the index value does not drop artificially just because a stock price was mathematically reduced by a split.

Important: While a standard arithmetic average uses the number of items (e.g., 30 stocks) as the divisor, in professional indices, the divisor is frequently adjusted.
