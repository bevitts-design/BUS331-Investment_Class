# Sealed Client Role-Play Packets

Print or copy one card at a time. Give it only to the designated student playing that client. The facts below come from the assigned BUS331 fictional client files; the reveal order is the teaching control.

## Team One

### Case 01 — Eleanor Vance

**Portrayal:** Thoughtful, measured, cautious. Preservation and income matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 72; retired librarian; $30,000 pension income; $1.2M net worth; high liquidity need for medical expenses; 2–5 year horizon; 4.5% target return; 6.0% standard deviation; risk averse.

**Teaching tension:** Medical liquidity takes priority over reaching for income.

### Case 02 — Marcus Chen

**Portrayal:** Ambitious, direct, optimistic about technology. Aggressive growth and early retirement matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 29; software engineer; $210,000 income; $150,000 net worth; concentrated technology-stock exposure; 11.0% target return; 22.0% standard deviation; risk seeking.

**Teaching tension:** Willingness for volatility does not remove concentration risk.

### Case 03 — David and Sarah Miller

**Portrayal:** Practical and collaborative. College funding and retirement both matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** ages 48 and 46; middle management; $165,000 combined income; $450,000 net worth; tuition payments begin in three years; 7.0% target return; 12.0% standard deviation; risk neutral.

**Teaching tension:** The nearer tuition need and retirement goal must be separated.

## Team Two

### Case 05 — Javier Rodriguez

**Portrayal:** Independent, experienced, and tax-aware. Diversification and a future business exit matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 55; business owner; $200,000 income; $3.0M net worth; illiquid business assets; tax-sensitive; 7.5% target return; 14.0% standard deviation; risk neutral.

**Teaching tension:** An attractive return goal must account for the existing illiquid business concentration.

### Case 07 — Linda Sterling

**Portrayal:** Careful and organized. Stability matters while planning a home purchase.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 51; marketing director; $95,000 income; $600,000 net worth; $150,000 cash need for a home purchase in 12 months; 5.5% target return; 9.0% standard deviation; risk averse.

**Teaching tension:** The known one-year cash need cannot be exposed to ordinary portfolio volatility.

### Case 08 — Robert Halloway

**Portrayal:** Determined and anxious about time. Catch-up retirement growth matters.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 58; retail manager; $70,000 income; $120,000 net worth; under-funded retirement; short horizon; 10.0% target return; 20.0% standard deviation; risk seeking.

**Teaching tension:** A desire to catch up may exceed financial capacity for loss.

## Team Three

### Case 04 — Chloe Dubois

**Portrayal:** Curious, new to investing, and future-focused. Long-term wealth building matters.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 24; graduate student; no current income; $2.5M net worth; novice experience; 30+ year horizon; 9.5% target return; 18.0% standard deviation; risk seeking.

**Teaching tension:** Long horizon and wealth do not erase the need to understand knowledge and willingness.

### Case 06 — Dr. Amara Okafor

**Portrayal:** Analytical and values-driven. Ethical growth matters.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 42; pediatrician; $280,000 income; $850,000 net worth; no tobacco, oil, or arms; 8.0% target return; 15.0% standard deviation; risk neutral.

**Teaching tension:** Values restrictions must become an implementable screen rather than a vague preference.

### Case 10 — Zoe Jenkins

**Portrayal:** Creative and candid about uneven income. Emergency reserves and growth matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 32; graphic designer; $65,000 income; $40,000 net worth; variable income; needs a 12-month cash reserve; 6.5% target return; 13.0% standard deviation; risk averse.

**Teaching tension:** The emergency reserve comes before the growth allocation.

## Team Four

### Case 11 — Thomas Murphy

**Portrayal:** Steady, cautious, and strongly loss-averse. Supplemental retirement matters.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 45; police officer; $85,000 income; $200,000 net worth; pension-backed; psychologically averse to losses; 4.0% target return; 5.0% standard deviation; risk averse.

**Teaching tension:** Pension support affects capacity, but willingness and loss aversion still matter.

### Case 12 — Yuki Tanaka

**Portrayal:** Globally minded and planning-oriented. Growth and diversification matter before an international move.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 38; technology consultant; $150,000 income; $300,000 net worth; USD/JPY currency exposure; moving in 10 years; 8.5% target return; 16.0% standard deviation; risk neutral.

**Teaching tension:** Relocation and currency exposure must be made explicit in the mandate.

### Case 14 — Elena Rossi

**Portrayal:** Sophisticated, efficient, and attentive to restrictions. Tax-efficient growth matters.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 52; corporate vice president; $450,000 income; $4.0M net worth; high tax bracket; SEC Rule 144 restrictions; 10.5% target return; 21.0% standard deviation; risk seeking.

**Teaching tension:** Sophistication and a high return target do not override legal and tax constraints.

## Team Five

### Case 09 — Arthur Sterling

**Portrayal:** Reflective and legacy-focused. Philanthropy and multigenerational wealth matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 80; retired executive; $50M+ net worth; multigenerational tax concerns; inflation protection; 5.0% target return; 7.0% standard deviation; risk neutral.

**Teaching tension:** Philanthropy, legacy, liquidity, taxes, and inflation may create competing priorities.

### Case 13 — The Petersons

**Portrayal:** Practical, stretched, and family-focused. Debt reduction and child education matter.

**Reveal only when asked:** ages 31 and 30; teacher and nurse; $110,000 income; $20,000 net worth; $180,000 student debt; low cash flow; 6.0% target return; 10.0% standard deviation; risk averse.

**Teaching tension:** Debt and cash-flow pressure may change whether investing is the next priority.

### Case 15 — Tyler Reed

**Portrayal:** Energetic and independent. Capital accumulation matters despite minimal starting capital.

**Reveal only when asked:** age 27; gig-economy worker; $55,000 income; $5,000 net worth; minimal starting capital; very long horizon; 9.0% target return; 19.0% standard deviation; risk seeking.

**Teaching tension:** A long horizon does not eliminate the effect of small starting capital and variable earnings.

## All cards: information-gap rule

If analysts ask about a fact not listed on the card, answer: **“That is not established in my brief.”** Do not invent a tax bracket, family detail, account registration, existing holding, product preference, exact cash-flow amount, or acceptable-loss figure.
