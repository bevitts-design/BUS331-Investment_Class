# Sealed Client Role-Play Packets

Print or copy one card at a time. Give it only to the designated student playing that client. The facts below come from the assigned BUS331 fictional client files; the reveal order is the teaching control.

## Team One

### Case 01 — Eleanor Vance

**Portrayal:** Thoughtful, measured, cautious. Preservation and income matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: Access for possible medical expenses comes before seeking additional income.
- If asked about risk comfort: A noticeable account decline would make you uneasy, even if income remained stable.
- If asked about timing: The amount and exact timing of possible medical expenses are not established.

**Teaching tension:** Medical liquidity takes priority over reaching for income.

### Case 02 — Marcus Chen

**Portrayal:** Ambitious, direct, optimistic about technology. Aggressive growth and early retirement matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: Early retirement matters more than maintaining exposure to any one exciting idea.
- If asked about risk comfort: You are optimistic about technology but recognize that a serious loss in one area would affect your confidence.
- If asked about existing exposure: The exact concentration is not established.

**Teaching tension:** Willingness for volatility does not remove concentration risk.

### Case 03 — David and Sarah Miller

**Portrayal:** Practical and collaborative. College funding and retirement both matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: The tuition need feels less flexible than retirement, but you want both goals protected.
- If asked about trade-offs: You would rather adjust a long-term goal than be forced to borrow for a near-term education payment.
- If asked about timing: The exact tuition schedule is not established.

**Teaching tension:** The nearer tuition need and retirement goal must be separated.

## Team Two

### Case 05 — Javier Rodriguez

**Portrayal:** Independent, experienced, and tax-aware. Diversification and a future business exit matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: You want the portfolio to reduce, not quietly repeat, the risks already tied to the business.
- If asked about taxes: You care about taxes around an eventual exit and prefer to avoid an unnecessary tax bill.
- If asked about timing: The exit timing and tax details are not established.

**Teaching tension:** An attractive return goal must account for the existing illiquid business concentration.

### Case 07 — Linda Sterling

**Portrayal:** Careful and organized. Stability matters while planning a home purchase.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: The home-purchase money must be dependable when needed.
- If asked about trade-offs: You would postpone a larger return opportunity rather than put the planned down payment at risk.
- If asked about timing: Whether the purchase date can move is not established.

**Teaching tension:** The known one-year cash need cannot be exposed to ordinary portfolio volatility.

### Case 08 — Robert Halloway

**Portrayal:** Determined and anxious about time. Catch-up retirement growth matters.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: You feel pressure to catch up for retirement and may be tempted by risk.
- If asked about risk comfort: A major setback could make the timeline feel harder to recover.
- If asked about resources or timing: The retirement date and other resources are not established.

**Teaching tension:** A desire to catch up may exceed financial capacity for loss.

## Team Three

### Case 04 — Chloe Dubois

**Portrayal:** Curious, new to investing, and future-focused. Long-term wealth building matters.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: You want to build wealth steadily and do not want to feel rushed into decisions you do not understand.
- If asked about risk comfort: You are new to investing and want to understand risk before accepting it.
- If asked about cash needs or experience: Your current spending needs and investment experience are not established beyond the baseline case.

**Teaching tension:** Long horizon and wealth do not erase the need to understand knowledge and willingness.

### Case 06 — Dr. Amara Okafor

**Portrayal:** Analytical and values-driven. Ethical growth matters.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: The values restrictions are non-negotiable, even if they limit choices.
- If asked about trade-offs: You would accept a narrower opportunity set rather than invest in a company that conflicts with your values.
- If asked about implementation detail: How you would prioritize among borderline industries is not established.

**Teaching tension:** Values restrictions must become an implementable screen rather than a vague preference.

### Case 10 — Zoe Jenkins

**Portrayal:** Creative and candid about uneven income. Emergency reserves and growth matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: Income can vary from month to month, so having accessible reserves reduces stress.
- If asked about risk comfort: You do not want to sell long-term investments during a slow work period.
- If asked about cash flow: The exact reserve amount and timing of income swings are not established.

**Teaching tension:** The emergency reserve comes before the growth allocation.

## Team Four

### Case 11 — Thomas Murphy

**Portrayal:** Steady, cautious, and strongly loss-averse. Supplemental retirement matters.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: The pension provides some support, but this account still needs to contribute to retirement security.
- If asked about risk comfort: Losses in this account would still feel difficult.
- If asked about loss limits: What loss would be tolerable is not established.

**Teaching tension:** Pension support affects capacity, but willingness and loss aversion still matter.

### Case 12 — Yuki Tanaka

**Portrayal:** Globally minded and planning-oriented. Growth and diversification matter before an international move.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: You want the move to be financially manageable without abandoning long-term growth.
- If asked about practical constraints: The future move could make currency changes matter more than they do today.
- If asked about planning details: The eventual location, expenses, and currency needs are not established.

**Teaching tension:** Relocation and currency exposure must be made explicit in the mandate.

### Case 14 — Elena Rossi

**Portrayal:** Sophisticated, efficient, and attentive to restrictions. Tax-efficient growth matters.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: You want growth to be tax-aware and must respect legal restrictions.
- If asked about trade-offs: You would not pursue a return opportunity that creates a compliance problem.
- If asked about implementation detail: The specific restricted holdings and tax details are not established.

**Teaching tension:** Sophistication and a high return target do not override legal and tax constraints.

## Team Five

### Case 09 — Arthur Sterling

**Portrayal:** Reflective and legacy-focused. Philanthropy and multigenerational wealth matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: Philanthropy and future generations both matter, while inflation should not quietly erode purchasing power.
- If asked about trade-offs: You want to be generous without creating uncertainty for future family needs.
- If asked about planning details: The giving schedule and estate structure are not established.

**Teaching tension:** Philanthropy, legacy, liquidity, taxes, and inflation may create competing priorities.

### Case 13 — The Petersons

**Portrayal:** Practical, stretched, and family-focused. Debt reduction and child education matter.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: Debt and monthly cash flow feel urgent, but you also care about future education costs.
- If asked about trade-offs: Reducing financial pressure now would make it easier to commit to a long-term plan later.
- If asked about planning details: The debt terms and education timeline are not established.

**Teaching tension:** Debt and cash-flow pressure may change whether investing is the next priority.

### Case 15 — Tyler Reed

**Portrayal:** Energetic and independent. Capital accumulation matters despite minimal starting capital.

**Reveal only when asked:**

- If asked about priorities: Long-term growth is motivating, but you also need a plan that works when income is uneven.
- If asked about practical constraints: The starting balance is small, so early mistakes would feel discouraging.
- If asked about cash flow: Your near-term reserve and exact earnings pattern are not established.

**Teaching tension:** A long horizon does not eliminate the effect of small starting capital and variable earnings.

## All cards: information-gap rule

If analysts ask about a fact not listed on the card, answer: **“That is not established in my brief.”** Do not invent a tax bracket, family detail, account registration, existing holding, product preference, exact cash-flow amount, or acceptable-loss figure.
