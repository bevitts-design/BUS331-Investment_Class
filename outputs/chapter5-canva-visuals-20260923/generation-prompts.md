## 01-holding-period-return

Title: Holding period return captures price change and income. Left: elegant investment flow diagram Beginning value → Ending value, plus separate dividend stream. Right formula: HPR = (Ending value − Beginning value + Income) / Beginning value. Footer: Measure the return over the full holding period. It is not automatically an annual return.

## 03-hpr-worked-answer

Title: Price gains and dividends produce a 75% holding period return. Three bold visual cards: Beginning value: 500 × $20 = $10,000. Ending value: 500 × $30 = $15,000. Dividend income: 500 × $0.50 × 10 = $2,500. Formula prominently: ($15,000 − $10,000 + $2,500) / $10,000 = 75%. Show result decomposition 50% price gain + 25% income return = 75%. Footer: 75% over five years—not 75% per year. Cash dividends are not assumed reinvested.

## 04-arithmetic-return

Title: Arithmetic return is the simple average. Left four equally weighted return tiles flowing into a sum then divided by number of periods. Right exact readable formula: Arithmetic average = (R₁ + R₂ + … + Rₜ) / T. Caption: T is the number of equal-length periods. Excel: =AVERAGE(B2:B5). Footer: Describes the average observed return per period; it does not measure compounded growth.

## 05-arithmetic-example

Title: Four annual returns average 11%. Four visually equal year cards: Year 1 +25%, Year 2 +10%, Year 3 +12%, Year 4 −3%. Formula: (25% + 10% + 12% − 3%) / 4 = 11%. Right large 11% with label Arithmetic average annual return. Bottom question: Did wealth grow at a constant 11% per year? Small answer: Use the geometric average to measure compounded growth.

## 06-multiperiod-compounding

Title: Multiply growth factors across time. Left horizontal five-stage timeline labeled Year 1 through Year 5 with growth factor (1 + R₁) through (1 + R₅), connected by multiplication signs. Right: Cumulative return = (1 + R₁) × (1 + R₂) × … × (1 + R₅) − 1. Three short steps: Convert each return to a growth factor. Multiply the growth factors. Subtract 1. Footer: This measures cumulative compounded return, assuming distributions are reinvested. Do not add annual returns.

## 07-geometric-return

Title: Geometric return measures compounded growth per period. Left starting wealth growing along a smooth path to ending wealth. Right exact formula: Geometric average = [(1 + R₁) × … × (1 + Rₜ)]^(1/T) − 1. Short explanation: The constant periodic rate that produces the same ending wealth. Small Excel panel: For returns in B2:B5, enter =1+B2 in C2 and fill down; then =GEOMEAN(C2:C5)-1. Footer: Use equal-length periods and reinvested total returns.

## 08-geometric-example

Title: The same returns produce about 10.6% compounded growth per year. Left wealth timeline: $100 → $125 → $137.50 → $154 → $149.38. Above arrows +25%, +10%, +12%, −3%. Right formula: (1.25 × 1.10 × 1.12 × 0.97)^(1/4) − 1 ≈ 10.6%. Bottom comparison: Arithmetic average 11% | Geometric average ≈ 10.6%. Footer: Compounding reflects the sequence of gains and losses in wealth; these annual returns are assumed reinvested.

## 09-four-distribution-measures

Title: Four measures describe different features of returns. Four clean visual rows with accurate schematic distributions at left and explanation at right. Mean: Center of the distribution; probability-weighted mean is expected return. Variance / standard deviation: Spread or volatility; standard deviation is the square root of variance. Skewness: Asymmetry; shows which tail extends farther. Kurtosis: Tail heaviness; helps describe extreme outcomes. Footer: Normal benchmark: raw kurtosis = 3; excess kurtosis = 0. Second footer tiny but legible: Standard deviation is derived from the second central moment. No claim kurtosis directly determines a tail probability.

## 10-interest-rate-perspectives

Title: One interest rate, three decision questions. Three spacious illustrated panels: Required return — What minimum return compensates me for time and risk? Discount rate — What are future cash flows worth today? Opportunity cost — What return do I give up on the best comparable alternative? Simple subtle shared central percentage symbol with arrows to panels. Footer: Compare alternatives with similar risk and timing. Original concise educational wording.
